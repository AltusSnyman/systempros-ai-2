## 2. The shortlist problem
**Customers choose from three names on a map.**
Most people looking for a plumber, a dentist or a detailer don't scroll through websites. They search, glance at the map, compare three businesses by their stars, photos and distance, and call one. If you're not in that group of three, you are not on the shortlist, however good your work is.
The work isn't about chasing traffic. It's about being present, credible and easy to choose at the exact moment someone nearby needs you.

## 3. Anatomy of a local search  (own diagram, Auckland example)
**Win the part of the page people actually use.**
A local search has three zones. Paid ads can sit on top. The map pack sits in the middle with names, stars, distance and a call button. Ordinary websites sit underneath, usually crowded out by directories and national brands.
Diagram labels (with benchmark share of clicks):
- **Ads** — about 4% of clicks
- **Map pack (top three on Google Maps)** — about 80% of clicks
- **Websites** — about 16% of clicks

## 5. The heatmap
**See where you rank, street by street.**
Your ranking isn't one number. Someone searching two streets away can see a different map from someone across town. A heatmap drops a grid of pins across your service area and checks where you appear at each one: green where you're in the top three, amber where you're close, red where customers can't see you.
We run one before Andrea starts and keep running them, so you can watch your area turn green instead of taking our word for it.
